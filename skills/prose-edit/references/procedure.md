# Prose editing procedure

Read the source text and its audience/purpose. Preserve factual meaning, commitments, names already present in the text, and the useful parts of the author’s voice. If an edit would change a factual claim, flag that change rather than silently adding confidence. Do not do new research or write a new piece under an editing request.

Tighten repetition, throat-clearing, unnecessary qualifiers, and abstract nouns. Prefer familiar words and active verbs. Use no em dashes in the delivered text. Replace one with a period, comma, or a simpler sentence as the meaning requires. Limit colons and chains of clauses; a short connected sentence often reads better. Do not replace every removed dash with a colon.

Remove AI-isms and decorative metaphors. Rewrite contrast formulas such as “not X, but Y” and “not that, this” into a direct statement of the point. Keep a deliberate author-specific reference when it serves the text or the user explicitly wants it; do not add decorative imagery of your own.

“Tighter” is the default. Preserve information the reader needs instead of optimizing only word count. “Warmer” means friendlier phrasing, a direct welcome, or more considerate instructions, not padding or effusive praise. Retain the requested format and meaningful structure.

Show the edited text first. If useful, add a short note only for a consequential meaning choice or unresolved fact. For file edits, use the requested file and summarize the change; do not commit unless asked. If user-approved wording intentionally differs from a default, preserve that choice.

## Original examples written for this package

**README introduction**

Before: “This is not just a dashboard, but a holistic command center that empowers users to seamlessly leverage their data.”

After: “Use the dashboard to explore your data and decide what to do next.”

**Warmer instructions**

Before: “Users are required to complete all fields prior to submission.”

After: “Fill in each field, then send the form.”

**Talk description**

Before: “In this session, we will delve into the various ways in which teams can improve collaboration: sharing context, reducing repeated work, and fostering alignment.”

After: “Learn how to share context and reduce repeated work so your team can make decisions together.”
